# The Machine Is the Game

A vision essay for v23. A proposal, not a changelog: what Hindsight Terminal becomes when the machine is the whole world and you never leave the house.

## 1. The turn

So far you have walked the world: the street, the bridge, the White House corridors. v23 turns it inside out. **You never leave the house.** You sit at a gutted laptop sealed to a table, and the whole planet comes to you through it: markets, streams, cameras, phone lines, leaked documents, satellite feeds, a presidency run over a video call. The outside world is still fully simulated (every person, price, war and grudge from v22 keeps running), but you only ever see it **through the machine**. That makes the game about one thing: what a single person can do to the world from one room, with a screen that sees tomorrow.

The genre this joins is the desktop thriller (a whole story told through one screen), but with a live, generated, consequential world behind the glass instead of a script.

## 2. Why the room is stronger than the street

- **Focus.** Walking was the weakest system: slow, fiddly on phones, the source of most of the playtest bugs. Interfaces are where the game is already strongest: the chart, the chat, the map, the feeds.
- **Tension.** A room you can't leave is claustrophobic in the right way. The phone rings. Someone knocks. The dog looks at the door. The world is enormous and you are very small inside it.
- **Power reads better at a distance.** A president who never leaves the house, ruling through a webcam and a stream, is a sharper satire than one walking corridors.
- **It all fits on a phone.** One screen, windows and tabs, no thumbsticks. It's equally at home on a laptop.

## 3. The desktop

The machine boots into a cracked, flickering desktop. Everything is a window:

- **TERMINAL.** The time chart: slide, jump, buy, sell, short, manipulate. The heart of the game.
- **FEEDS.** Live cams of city centres around the world (the WORLD CAMS from v22, now full windows): crowds, protests, ruins, your posters, your statues. Each frame is generated from the simulation, so you can see your choices land.
- **KLIK.** You stream from the desk, with the room behind you. Chat is the generated grammar that reacts to everything, and it is the world's loudest mirror. Leaks happen when you forget what is on the other monitor.
- **CHIRP and THE NET.** Social posts, WORLDPEDIA, FACEPLACE, the rich list. People post about you, then people post about the people who posted.
- **PHONE.** Calls come in: a rival, a leader, a reporter, your mum, the man under the bridge, the president you bought. Every call is the procedural, skill-based confrontation, done by voice instead of face to face: temper, anger and motive, three approaches drawn from a pool, GUT reading the room.
- **EMPIRE.** Countries, companies, the war department, the map. Buy, invade, sanction, nuke.
- **OVAL (remote).** Win an election and the presidency arrives as a window: a video call with your cabinet, the in-tray, the press briefing you give from your kitchen, the situation room as a grid of feeds. You guide, they act, and the world reacts to the guidance.
- **AGENTS.** People you hire to be your hands outside: a fixer, a lawyer, a hacker, a bodyguard, a stand-in for public appearances. You give orders; they come back changed, or don't come back. The street game lives on as their stories.
- **THE MACHINE.** Its own health, world stats, the power ranking, the money supply, the dead. It wears out on your hands.

## 4. The house around the screen

The room is the only physical space, and it reacts. The window shows the world getting worse or better. The dog sleeps or paces. Post piles up, and some of it ticks. The TV in the corner runs the shows made about you. Knocks at the door are events: police, a fan, a process server, an assassin, your old self. The substances still live in the cupboard, and salvia still opens a whole other game. Your body is the meter you can't trade away: hunger, sleep, health, age, paranoia.

## 4b. One chair, many rooms

The constant is **you, sat down, at the machine.** Where the desk is changes everything around it. Your life decides where you end up sitting, and each place reskins the room, changes the rules, and changes what reaches the screen:

- **The house in the field.** The start: a crate, a car battery, the dog. Full access, no protection.
- **A prison cell.** Arrested? You still have the machine, smuggled in and taped under the bunk. One hour of screen time a day, guards walking past, a cellmate who wants in. Bribe the guard for longer sessions. The feeds can show your own trial.
- **A cabin in the woods.** Hiding, or HUNTED. A slow satellite connection, no KLIK (it would give away where you are), very good for watching other people.
- **A bunker.** After the bombs. The feeds are mostly green. The phone rings less. The machine is the last bright thing on Earth.
- **The Oval Office.** President, but still sat at the same gutted laptop on a much better desk. The cabinet is on the other screens. The leaks are worse.
- **A hospital bed, a yacht, a tower penthouse, a lock-up under the arches, an orbital station, a ship between planets.** Each is a desk with its own view, its own interruptions and its own limits.

Moving between them is the story: you don't walk there, you are taken, you flee, or you buy your way in, and the next scene opens with you sitting down again. The room is the level, and the interface is the game.

## 5. Agents instead of legs

You don't punch anyone in v23. You **order** it, or pay for it, or prevent it. Each agent is a procedural person with loyalty, competence, a temper and a life of their own. Send the fixer to buy a judge and you get a report back: maybe the judge, maybe a photo of your fixer in handcuffs, maybe a fixer who has started working for someone else. The consequences system writes their stories, and the credits remember them. Strategy shifts from aim to judgement: who you trust, how much you tell them, what you can see on the feeds to check their work.

## 6. The presidency, remote

Being president is the clearest expression of the new shape. You never go to Washington. The office comes to the machine: a cabinet on a video call, a press corps watching your living room, a situation room of six feeds. Decisions are windows with a timer. The leaks are worse because you are always on camera. The power is greater because the machine sees tomorrow, and the people around you start to wonder how you always know.

## 7. Replay forever, still

Everything from v22 stays: real money and inflation, the currency vacuum, consequences on named people, eras and planets, 210 endings, the machine wearing out. The room changes with the era too. In 2026 it is a laptop on a crate; in 2300 it is a pod on an orbital station with the same dog; in deep time it is a shrine around a machine nobody remembers building. The house is the constant. Everything outside it is never the same twice.

## 8. What changes, concretely

- The title opens on the room and the boot screen, not the bridge.
- The desk travels: house, prison cell, cabin, bunker, Oval Office and beyond (section 4b). Each place is a reskin with its own rules, and you always arrive sitting down.
- The street, the house walk and the field are removed as places you control. Their systems keep running as simulation, and their stories reach you through feeds, calls, posts and agents.
- New windows: FEEDS, PHONE, AGENTS, OVAL (remote).
- Existing panels (desk, KLIK, EMPIRE, MEDIA, THE NET, the machine) become windows on one desktop that you can tile on a laptop or swipe between on a phone.
- Controls: tap or click windows; keys for everything (as v22).

## 9. The speedrun playtest that decides it

Before building, play the current game as a speedrunner for five minutes, never leaving the desk, and count:

- how much of the fun already happens at the machine,
- what forced you to stand up and walk,
- how long the first $1M, the first country and the presidency take,
- where the screen felt empty or confusing.

The results are in section 10, written straight after the run.

## 10. The speedrun, analysed frame by frame

I played Boba, the Gambler, as a speedrunner: no exploring, keys only, never leaving the desk. Then I played the first ten seconds again, slowly, to see what actually happened. A run nobody would believe if you described it:

- **0 to 4 seconds.** Seven taps through the bridge intro. The game is on the desk at 4 seconds, which is good.
- **5 seconds, trade one.** OBOL's lowest price within reach is 50.7 times below today. Jump back, B, jump forward, S. **$1,000 becomes $50,730 in about a second.** Heat goes from 0 to 30.
- **5 seconds, trade two.** The same trade, the same quarter, the same ×50.7. **$2.57M.** The market's memory of my first order didn't touch the second, because $50K is nothing against OBOL's depth. Heat 60.
- **5 seconds.** Cassius Vane interrupts: "WHO IS IN MY HOUSE." A good scene, arriving at the worst moment: the second time I pressed a key.
- **9 seconds.** First million, after five trades.
- **10 seconds.** An agent and six vehicles. "Step away from the keyboard." The top option is **Run.**, a GUT 10 check the Gambler (GUT 4) almost always fails, and failing is death in prison. I pressed the top option, like most people do.
- **15 seconds.** Dead, with $25.1M. The machine had 12% of its life used. My character existed for 11 seconds of game.
- **15 to 300 seconds.** Reborn in 2048 and left alone at the pick screen. Nothing came to find me. With no life running, the world does not tick, so the room was silent for four and a half minutes.

What this means, without excuses:

1. **The time machine has no friction at small sizes.** Depth and ripple only bite on big orders, so the first ten trades are free money. The strategy described in the design essay starts too late. **Fix:** every round trip on the same pair should scar it (the market "remembers" a buyer who is always right), and repeated wins should raise flux cost and SEC suspicion sharply, so you have to vary assets, timing and size from the very first minute.
2. **Heat outruns the player.** Thirty heat per trade gives an arrest in about six trades, roughly ten seconds. Losing should take minutes of greed, not seconds of play. **Fix:** heat scaled to profit relative to the market (a quiet $50K is invisible), a warning card at 70 (EVERCLEAR is already built for this), and the arrest only once you have been told.
3. **The first option kills you.** The top option is the one people tap without reading. **Fix:** order options by safety, show the odds on every check (the desk already does this), and make Run fail into HUNTED rather than straight to death the first time.
4. **Story beats interrupt the hands.** Vane, the agent and the bookie arrived within six seconds of each other. **Fix:** a queue. One beat per minute at most, and never while a key is held down.
5. **Dead air when nobody is alive.** Between lives the room should still be a room: the feeds run, the phone rings, the news says what your death did.

The diagnosis for v23 follows from this: **the machine is already the fun, and it is too generous with it.** Make the machine harder to exploit and more watchful, and give the world time to look back at you.

## 11. The watcher and the watched: a Beholder-shaped machine

There is a better way to frame "the machine is the whole game": **surveillance.** Games about the watcher (a landlord with cameras in every flat, reporting tenants to the state) are about the slow corruption of a decent person through small daily choices. Hindsight's machine is the ultimate surveillance tool. It doesn't just see what people do. It sees what they **will** do.

**The format.** Each in-game day is one sitting at the machine, with a clock on the wall:

- **The building.** You own or rent out the flats around the house (they already exist in the street, locked or not). Tenants are procedural people with jobs, tempers, secrets and futures. Each has a **dossier** window you fill in as you learn things.
- **The tools.** Cameras and bugs placed by your agents, phone taps, KLIK and CHIRP scraping, bank feeds, and the machine itself, which shows you a tenant's tomorrow for flux. Every tool leaves traces, and some tenants look for them.
- **The ministry.** The state (the agency, the SEC, the president you bought or the one you replaced) sends **directives**: report anyone hoarding cash, anyone talking about the machine, anyone who has been to the bridge. Quotas, deadlines, rewards and threats. The TV runs their propaganda.
- **The choices.** Report a tenant and get paid; protect them and risk being reported yourself; blackmail them; warn them; use what you saw to trade against them. Everything feeds the consequence system: the woman you reported is taken away, and her son posts about it. The man you warned leaves a cake at your door, or a camera.
- **The watched watcher.** You are being surveilled too. Someone else has a machine, or a better feed, or your old FACEPLACE posts. Paranoia becomes a meter. Ilse, the Paranoid, gets the best tools and the worst sleep.
- **The family in the room.** The dog, the fridge, the rent, your body. Small needs that push you into bigger compromises, in the tradition of the genre.

**Why this fits.** It turns the hyper-reactive rule into the core loop: everything you learn is current, specific and about a named person, and every decision lands on someone. It gives the machine a daily rhythm (open the feeds, read the dossiers, answer the ministry, trade on what you know, close the curtains). And it asks the question the whole game has been circling: **what would you do to people if you could see their futures?**

## 12. Order of work for v23

1. Tune the first ten minutes (section 10, fixes 1 to 4): the speedrun should take an hour, not fifteen seconds.
2. The desktop shell: windows for TERMINAL, FEEDS, KLIK, CHIRP and THE NET, PHONE, EMPIRE, THE MACHINE.
3. The building, dossiers and the ministry (section 11). This is the new daily loop.
4. PHONE calls as the skill-based confrontations, AGENTS as your hands outside.
5. OVAL as a remote window. The street retires as a place you walk, and lives on as simulation and stories.
6. Play it again as a speedrunner. The target: first million around minute five, first arrest only after a clear warning, and no second without the world reacting.
